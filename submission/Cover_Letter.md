Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Can Satellite Vegetation Data Attribute War-Time Environmental Damage to a Single Event? A Difference-in-Differences Test of the Kakhovka Dam Destruction", for publication as a Research Article in *Environment and Security*.

Satellite imagery is increasingly offered as evidence of conflict-related environmental harm, and a standalone crime of ecocide has been proposed for the Rome Statute. This study asks whether a counterfactual design can attribute vegetation change to one dated act of war — the destruction of the Kakhovka Dam in June 2023 — by comparing Kherson Oblast with four Romanian counties and subjecting the estimate to placebo tests, an event study, randomization inference and a control-only divergence check. It finds a statistically significant, seasonally robust relative decline in Kherson's vegetation after the event, but also that the design cannot attribute it to the dam: one control region shifts by as much and pre-event trends deviate. This distinction between a significant satellite signal and an event-attributable one is, I believe, useful to the journal's readers because it shows, with fully reproducible code, which conditions make satellite evidence of war-time environmental damage hold up or fail.

An earlier version of this work, with results superseded by corrections described in the manuscript, was posted on EarthArXiv [preprint DOI]. The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.
